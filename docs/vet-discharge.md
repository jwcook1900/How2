# Vet discharge guides — why this exists

North star, straight from the clinic: **"People don't listen to me. They're
distracted."** Everything in the discharge flow exists to make sure owners
actually understand and follow the care plan once they leave the clinic.

## Origin

James showed Whiskey's GotIt Guide to his vet (Dr Hunt) and a senior nurse at
the end of a consultation about Whiskey's ongoing care (idiopathic epilepsy,
medication three times a day, chronic lip fold dermatitis, environmental
seizure triggers). Unprompted, they stopped talking about pet sitters and
started talking about their own workflow: could this carry **discharge
instructions**? The conversation became implementation, not validation — the
strongest signal there is. The clinic has agreed to share real discharge
templates for evaluation, and James has already converted Whiskey's genuine
discharge notes into a guide in under a minute.

## The problems the clinic described

- **Owners don't absorb discharge information.** They're emotional and
  overwhelmed after surgery; verbal instructions, printed sheets and med
  advice mostly don't stick.
- **Reception and nurses burn time on repeat questions** that were covered at
  discharge: How often is this medication? Is this behaviour normal? When is
  the next tablet? Can they eat yet? Should the wound look like this?
- **Existing discharge documentation isn't engaging** — printed sheets, PDFs,
  long emails. "If it's in that nice, friendly, easy-to-read format, people
  will read it."

## The workflow to optimise (clinic as creator)

Clinic writes its normal discharge notes → uploads PDF / photo / pasted text
→ AI converts to a structured guide → clinic reviews (medications and advice
confirmed against the paperwork) → shares by SMS / email / WhatsApp → owner
holds a permanent, mobile-friendly guide with reminders, photos and videos.

The clinic must never duplicate work or learn a new documentation system:
the guide is generated from what they already produce today.

## Design philosophy (binding)

- The clinic remains the author. AI transforms existing information into a
  better experience; it never makes clinical decisions and never invents
  clinical details — anything missing or ambiguous is flagged for human
  confirmation ("⚠️ Check with your clinic: …"), not inferred.
- Calm, reassuring, effortless. Medications and emergency warning signs are
  visually prioritised; the veterinary-advice disclaimer is always present.
- Clinic-distributed guides raise the security bar: server-verified edit
  access, unguessable links, and (Tier 2) locked-by-default with the code on
  the discharge paperwork.
